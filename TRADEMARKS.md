# TCJ Trademark and Brand Policy

This policy explains how the names, logos, package identifiers, and other brand assets associated with **TCJ Framework** may be used.

The TCJ Framework source code is licensed separately under the [GNU Lesser General Public License v3.0 only](LICENSE.txt). That software license grants copyright permissions to use, study, modify, and redistribute the covered code under its terms. It does **not** grant a general license to use TCJ names, logos, or other source-identifying marks in a way that suggests an official TCJ release, sponsorship, affiliation, or endorsement.

This policy is intended to protect users from confusion about which software, packages, documentation, and services come from the TCJ project. It does not reduce the permissions granted by the LGPL.

## TCJ marks

For this policy, the **TCJ Marks** include, to the extent protected by applicable law:

- `TCJ`;
- `TCJ Framework`;
- the TCJ Framework logo, icon, and banner artwork;
- official TCJ package and distribution names, including the `TCJ.*` NuGet naming family;
- confusingly similar names, logos, or visual identities when used in a way likely to suggest an official TCJ origin.

No use of `®` is claimed by this policy. Registration status may vary by jurisdiction and over time.

## Uses that are generally allowed

You may use the TCJ Marks without separate written permission for truthful, non-confusing references to the project, including:

- stating that an application is **built with TCJ Framework**, **uses TCJ Framework**, or is **compatible with TCJ Framework**;
- linking to the official TCJ Framework repository, documentation, releases, or NuGet packages;
- referring to TCJ Framework in articles, books, training material, conference talks, reviews, comparisons, issue reports, and technical discussions;
- displaying the unmodified TCJ name or logo when accurately identifying the official project, provided the presentation does not imply endorsement of an unrelated product or organization;
- naming a community group or resource in a descriptive way, such as `TCJ Framework Users`, when it is clear that the resource is community-run and not an official TCJ project channel.

Applications built with TCJ Framework may use **any product name, company name, logo, visual identity, or commercial brand chosen by their authors**. This policy does not require an application to use TCJ branding.

## Modified versions and forks

The LGPL permits modification and redistribution of the covered source code. This policy does not take that right away.

If you distribute a materially modified fork, derivative distribution, or repackaged version of TCJ Framework:

- do not present it as an official TCJ Framework release;
- use a distinct primary product/distribution name and visual identity when necessary to avoid confusion;
- do not use the official TCJ logo or banner as the primary branding of the modified distribution without written permission;
- do not publish modified or unofficial packages under the official `TCJ.*` package identity in a way that suggests they are maintained or released by the TCJ project;
- you may truthfully state that the work is **based on TCJ Framework**, **forked from TCJ Framework**, or **compatible with TCJ Framework**;
- preserve all copyright, license, source, and modification notices required by the applicable software license.

Renaming code, namespaces, assemblies, or package identifiers as part of a fork is a software modification governed by the LGPL. The restriction here is not on making that modification; it is on using the TCJ Marks in a confusing or misleading way after the modification.

## Permission is required

Please obtain written permission before using a TCJ Mark:

- as the name of a company, product, hosted service, commercial framework, domain name, or unrelated software project;
- as the primary branding of a modified TCJ distribution;
- in a way that implies official status, certification, sponsorship, partnership, or endorsement;
- on merchandise or promotional goods whose primary purpose is to trade on the TCJ brand;
- in an official-looking package feed, marketplace listing, or distribution channel that could reasonably be mistaken for one operated by the TCJ project;
- in a logo or visual mark that is confusingly similar to the official TCJ logo.

## Logo and visual identity

When using the official TCJ logo to identify the official project:

- do not distort its proportions;
- do not recolor, redraw, rotate, crop, or combine it with another logo in a way that creates a new composite mark;
- keep enough clear space around it to remain recognizable;
- do not use it as your application's own logo or as the primary identity of an unrelated product;
- do not imply that the TCJ project designed, certified, sponsors, or endorses your product unless that is actually true.

You may create your own application branding even when the application uses TCJ Framework internally.

## Package and namespace references

Using official package identifiers such as `TCJ.Core` in dependency manifests, documentation, compatibility statements, logs, or other technically necessary references is allowed.

Publishing a modified package under an official `TCJ.*` identity may create confusion about origin and maintenance. Use a distinct package identity for unofficial distributions unless you have written permission from the TCJ project to use the official package naming family.

## No endorsement

Use of TCJ Framework or a truthful reference to the TCJ Marks does not imply sponsorship, endorsement, certification, partnership, or affiliation with the TCJ project.

If this policy and the LGPL appear to conflict about copying, modifying, or redistributing the software itself, the LGPL controls those copyright permissions; this policy applies only to rights associated with the TCJ Marks and prevention of misleading source identification.

## Questions and permissions

For brand-use questions or permission requests, open a GitHub Discussion or repository issue before publishing the proposed branded use. Do not include confidential information in a public issue.
